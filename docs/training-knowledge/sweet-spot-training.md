# Sweet Spot Training

Distilled principles for sweet spot training — probably the single most
productive intensity band for time-crunched cyclists. Used to ground the AI
weekly-plan prompt when the rider's goal is FTP improvement or building
aerobic fitness efficiently.

---

## What is Sweet Spot?

Sweet spot sits at **84–97% of FTP** — between upper Zone 3 (Tempo) and
lower Zone 4 (Threshold). The term was coined in 2005 by coach Frank Overton
(FasCat Coaching) after analysis of power-based training models.

The concept: training effect (fitness gain) and physiological strain (fatigue)
both increase with intensity — but strain increases faster. The "sweet spot" is
the point on the curve where training effect is near-maximum but strain is still
manageable. Beyond this zone, fatigue accumulates faster than fitness.

---

## Why Sweet Spot beats Zone 2 for time-crunched athletes

- **Zone 2**: builds aerobic base but requires long durations (2h+) to be
  meaningfully effective. Most recreational cyclists don't have that time.
- **Sweet Spot**: produces the same or better aerobic adaptations in shorter
  sessions (45–90 min). More "physiological adaptations per hour" than Z2.
- **Zone 4+**: maximally effective but creates heavy fatigue, limiting
  training frequency. Hard to sustain more than 1–2 sessions/week.

Sweet spot is the best zone for riders with 60–90 min per session who can
train 3–5 days/week.

## Sweet Spot session structures

### Classic 2×20 (most common)
- 2 reps × 20 minutes at 88–94% FTP
- Recovery: 5 min easy spin between reps
- Total hard time: 40 min
- Good for: intermediate riders, FTP building, base phase

### 3×15
- 3 reps × 15 minutes at 88–94% FTP
- Recovery: 5 min between reps
- Slightly easier to pace than 20-min blocks; good entry point
- Good for: beginners to sweet spot, shorter sessions

### 4×10
- 4 reps × 10 minutes at 90–95% FTP
- Recovery: 3–4 min between reps
- Shorter reps allow slightly higher intensity
- Good for: transition from Tempo to Threshold; adding variety

### 1×30 "sustained sweet spot"
- Single 30-min block at 84–90% FTP
- No recovery breaks (continuous)
- Good for: experienced riders, building mental toughness

---

## Adaptation timeline

| Weeks of consistent sweet spot training | Expected effect |
|----------------------------------------|----------------|
| 1–3 weeks | Adapting; may feel hard at first |
| 4–6 weeks | Power becomes more sustainable; less perceived effort |
| 8–12 weeks | Meaningful FTP gains visible on re-test |
| 16 weeks (FasCat base block) | Full aerobic base built; ready for more specific intensity |

---

## When to use Sweet Spot in a periodized plan

- **Base phase** (early in a cycle): Primary intensity block alongside Z2 Foundation
- **Build phase**: Use alongside Threshold Development and VO2 intervals
- **Recovery week**: Remove sweet spot; replace with Z1–Z2 only
- **Peak/Race phase**: Reduce SS volume; add more Z4/Z5 specificity

---

## Sweet Spot vs other workouts: decision guide

| If the rider... | Use Sweet Spot? |
|----------------|----------------|
| Is time-crunched (45–75 min sessions) | YES — more effective than Z2 |
| Has 2+ hours/session | Z2 Foundation also valid; mix both |
| Is in first weeks of structured training | Start with Tempo (Z3) → progress to SS |
| Is in build phase and experienced | Add Threshold (Z4) blocks too |
| Is in recovery week | NO — rest or Z1 only |
| Just did a hard race or VO2 session | Give 48h before next SS session |

---

## Key rule for AI plan generation

**Sweet spot is not sprint training and not threshold intervals.** It's sustained
sub-threshold effort. Sessions should have long blocks (10–30 min) at 84–97%,
with short recovery between reps. Prescribing 30-second efforts as "sweet spot"
is wrong — those are interval/anaerobic work.
